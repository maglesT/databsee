## The video
- Make a **calm, step-by-step tutorial video** (not an ad) for **Medhanta TIDE**, recruitment with online tests and live quizzes for pharma companies in India (formerly called "Recruitment"): openings (requisitions) and approval, online applications and CVs, online tests with automatic marking, a live quiz in the room (projector plus phones), shortlisting with a cut-off, interviews with panel feedback, offers approved by the manager, GMP qualification checks, hire with a hand-over record, training records signed with an e-signature and read by TrueBatch, candidate data rights under India's DPDP Act, history and access log.
- Use the name **"Medhanta TIDE"** in every title card and every caption (as written in the table below). Never call the product "Recruitment" or "Recruitment by Medhanta".
- Audience: plant heads, HR, QA, production and engineering managers, and buyers. Plain language; explain each term in about three words: **requisition** – request to hire · **GMP** – pharma quality rules · **competent technical staff** – qualified lab staff · **shortlist** – next-round list · **cut-off** – pass mark · **SOP** – standard operating procedure · **QMS** – quality system (Medhanta ASSURE) · **e-signature** – signing with your password · **TrueBatch** – Medhanta's electronic batch record · **DPDP Act** – India's data protection law · **hand-over record** – file for personnel and training.
- Format: **16:9, 1920×1080**. Target length **3–6 minutes** (plan ≈ 5 min 44 s).
- Story: One working day of hiring at the fictional **Medhanta Demo Plant, Unit 2**: the QC lab needs two **QC Analysts** and Production needs four **Production Operators**. A requisition is approved, candidates apply, QC candidates take an online test, Production candidates take a live quiz in the room (projector and phones side by side), the best go to interviews, and one QC Analyst is offered the job, checked against the GMP requirements and hired. The same day, an operator's SOP training is recorded and signed, for TrueBatch to read. A candidate also uses her data rights. All people, candidates, employers, SOPs, emails and phone numbers are fictional demo data.

## PRONUNCIATION RULES FOR THE VOICE-OVER (apply to every scene; captions keep normal spelling)

1. Speak naturally, at one steady pace (about 130 words a minute). Never slow down, pause or stretch on abbreviations, numbers or brand names.
2. Letter abbreviations are said as quick, joined letters in ONE beat, like a newsreader, never spelled out slowly with gaps: US = "you-ess" (one quick word), EU = "ee-you", UK = "you-kay", FDA = "eff-dee-ay", QA = "cue-ay", QMS = "cue-em-ess", SOP = "ess-oh-pee", OEE = "oh-ee-ee", IQ / OQ / PQ = "eye-cue", "oh-cue", "pee-cue", MIT = "em-eye-tee", DPDP = "dee-pee-dee-pee", AI = "ay-eye", CEO = "see-ee-oh", CFO = "see-eff-oh". Never read the dots in "U.S." or "e.g."; say "you-ess" and "for example".
3. Abbreviations that are said as WORDS: CAPA = "KAP-uh", SHRM = "shurm", Ipca = "IP-kuh".
4. Web addresses and domains: "." is said "dot", and the ending is said as a word: ".in" = "dot in" (rhymes with "pin"), ".com" = "dot com", ".co.in" = "dot co dot in". Never spell an ending letter by letter ("dot i-n" is wrong).
5. Brand and product names (confirm with Medhanta before final recording): Medhanta = "meh-DHAAN-tah", Batchwright = "BATCH-rite", Digi = "DIJ-ee" (as in "digital"; "Digi partner" = "DIJ-ee partner", never "DIG-ee"), McKinsey = "muh-KIN-zee", Ranbaxy = "ran-BAK-see", Alexion = "uh-LEK-see-on", Baddi = "BUD-dee", LEAD = "leed", DIVE = "dive", RISE = "rise", TIDE = "tide" (one word, like the sea tide; never spelled out), ASSURE = "uh-SHOOR", EQUIP = "ee-KWIP", QLIMS = "cue-lims", MMS = "em-em-ess", TrueBatch = "TROO-batch".
6. Indian money and numbers, said the Indian way: "₹16 lakh" = "sixteen lakh rupees" (lakh = "laak", rhymes with "rock"), "₹6 crore" = "six crore" (crore = "krore"), "US$4,700" = "forty-seven hundred dollars", "US$10–25 million" = "ten to twenty-five million dollars", "600-batch" = "six-hundred-batch", "60%" = "sixty percent", "2×" = "twice". Never read symbols aloud (₹, $, %, ×, –) as symbol names.
7. Footnote numbers (¹ ² ³) are on screen only. Never say them.
8. If a word could be misread, write it in the voice-over text the way it sounds (for example "DIJ-ee partner", "you-ess", "dot in"), keeping the correct spelling only in the captions.
